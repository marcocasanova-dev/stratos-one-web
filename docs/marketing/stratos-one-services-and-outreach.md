# Stratos One — Services, target audience and outreach (working draft)

Internal planning document, not website copy. It follows the website brief:
no invented customers, results, prices, team size or registration. The target
audience below is a **hypothesis to test in conversations**, not an established
customer base.

## 1. What Stratos One offers

Stratos One builds AI agents and software integrations that handle enquiries,
collect information and coordinate follow-up across a business's existing tools,
with senior engineering oversight and clear handoffs to people.

**Starting offer: enquiry handling.** Incoming enquiry → collect details →
answer approved questions → create a structured request → notify the team →
prepare follow-up. One channel, one workflow, a limited set of integrations.

### The four packages (all scoped by proposal, no public prices)

| Package | What the customer gets | Typical outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow Assessment | A useful workflow identified, existing tools reviewed, scope, expected value and success measures defined | Process map, integration inventory, feasibility review, scoped proposal |
| Agent Pilot | One workflow tested with limited integrations, clear boundaries and measurable results | Bounded implementation, evaluation examples, pilot review |
| Production Implementation | Workflow connected to the customer's systems, failure scenarios tested, monitoring and handover set up | Production integrations, documented permissions, operational monitoring, handover |
| Managed Operations | Monthly service: maintenance, monitoring, failure review, agreed improvements | Agreed monitoring, maintenance, reporting, defined improvement allowance; support and usage allowances in the proposal |

### What is out of scope (do not promise)

- A staffed call centre or round-the-clock human operators.
- Unlimited changes, unlimited usage or 24/7 support.
- Guaranteed response times, appointments, savings or revenue.
- A finished SaaS product. Consider SaaS only after several customers pay for
  substantially the same workflow.

## 2. Who to target (hypothesis)

**Primary hypothesis: small and mid-sized service businesses with repetitive
enquiries**, especially technical services where a request needs a few details
before someone can respond.

Examples to test (each is an assumption, not a customer):

- Heating, plumbing, electrical and HVAC installation and maintenance firms.
- Facility-management and building-services companies.
- Property managers handling tenant requests.
- Equipment service and repair businesses.
- Small IT or managed-service providers handling support requests.

**Signs a business is a good fit**

- Enquiries arrive through several channels (phone, email, web form, messaging).
- Staff repeatedly ask the same follow-up questions to complete a request.
- Requests are copied by hand into a CRM, ticket system or spreadsheet.
- Someone decides each time who should respond.
- The business has a few tools in place that can be connected, and the owner or
  an operations lead can approve changes.

**Signs of a poor fit (for now)**

- Highly regulated or sensitive data in every request, without appetite for a
  privacy review.
- No existing tools to integrate and no one to own the process.
- Expectation of a fully unattended call centre.
- Very high enquiry volume that needs an established product rather than a pilot.

## 3. How to reach companies

### Principles

1. **Learn first, sell second.** The plan is 10–15 conversations to validate the
   workflow and audience. Ask how they handle enquiries today before pitching.
2. **One clear ask:** a short conversation about one workflow, not a demo of
   everything.
3. **Be honest about the stage:** a new studio, founder-led, looking for a
   bounded paid pilot. Do not imply a customer base, team or registered company.

### Channels

| Channel | How to use it | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal network and referrals | Ask people who run or work with service businesses for an introduction | Usually the best first conversations |
| LinkedIn (Marco's profile) | Share engineering examples and the launch post; message relevant owners or operations leads with a personal, specific note | Use the company page for the offer, the profile for conversation |
| Local business groups | Trade associations, chambers of commerce, craft and trade guilds, regional founder and business meetups in Munich and Bavaria | Meet owners in person; ask to listen, not pitch |
| Calls to local businesses | Short, specific conversation about how they handle enquiries | See compliance note below |
| Content | Short posts or write-ups of the enquiry workflow (illustrative, sample data only) | Builds credibility without claiming customers |
| Website | The demo and contact form on stratos-one.eu | Only useful once people have a reason to visit |

### Suggested first message (adapt, keep it short)

> Hello [name], I'm Marco, founder of Stratos One in Munich. I'm talking with
> service businesses about how they handle incoming enquiries: collecting the
> missing details, entering them in a system, deciding who responds. I'm
> exploring whether a focused AI workflow could help. Could I ask you three
> questions about how it works at [company] today? No sales pitch.

### Conversation questions (validation)

1. How do enquiries reach you today, and through which channels?
2. What details do you usually have to ask for before someone can respond?
3. Where do the details end up (inbox, spreadsheet, CRM, ticket tool)?
4. Who decides who responds, and how long does that take?
5. What happens outside office hours?
6. Which tools do you already use?
7. What would make you hesitant to let software handle first contact?
8. Have you tried automating this before? What happened?

### Compliance note (Germany/EU) — check before outreach

Unsolicited marketing by email, and to a lesser extent by phone, is restricted
under German and EU rules (for example UWG §7 and GDPR). Cold email to business
addresses generally needs prior consent or a clear legal basis; B2B phone calls
need at least presumed consent. LinkedIn messages and in-person contact are
different channels with their own rules. This is not legal advice: confirm what
is allowed with a lawyer or chamber of commerce before running any outreach at
scale, and keep a record of consent and opt-outs.

## 4. Validation milestones (from the brief)

1. Speak with 10–15 prospective businesses.
2. Seek a bounded paid pilot.
3. Measure outcomes before claiming savings: time spent on enquiries,
   completeness of collected information, handoff quality, exception rate,
   operating cost and support effort, against a real baseline.
4. Aim for three paying customers with substantially similar needs before
   investing in a generalised SaaS platform.
